# Truth, Framing, and the Tabloid Gaze: An Analysis of *Amanda Knox* (2016)

**Assignment type:** Film analysis essay
**Subject:** *Amanda Knox* (dir. Rod Blackhurst & Brian McGinn, 2016, 92 min, Netflix)

---

## 1. Introduction

*Amanda Knox* (2016) is a 92-minute American documentary directed by Rod Blackhurst and Brian McGinn, written by Matthew Hamachek and McGinn, and released by Netflix on 30 September 2016 after premiering at the Toronto International Film Festival on 10 September 2016. It reconstructs the 2007 murder of Meredith Kercher in Perugia, the investigation and trials that followed, and the eventual acquittal of Amanda Knox and Raffaele Sollecito by the Italian Supreme Court of Cassation after Knox had spent four years in prison.

The film is usually discussed as a "true crime" documentary, but its real subject is not the murder. It is the machinery that turned a twenty-year-old exchange student into a global media character before any verdict existed. This essay argues that the film's achievement is rhetorical rather than investigative: it explains *how* a narrative was built around Knox, while largely declining to interrogate the evidentiary record that narrative was built on. That choice makes the film effective as an argument about media and much weaker as an account of the case.

## 2. Structure and the strategy of the four voices

The film's most deliberate formal decision is its cast of interviewees: Knox herself, her former boyfriend Sollecito, the prosecutor Giuliano Mignini, and Nick Pisa, the *Daily Mail* reporter who covered the case.

This is a four-corner structure, and each corner does a distinct job. Knox and Sollecito supply the experience of being narrated by others — the sense of watching your own life turned into a story you cannot edit. Mignini supplies the institutional logic: the certainty, the moral framing, the conviction that the behaviour of the accused was itself evidence of guilt. Pisa supplies the commercial logic: the admission, delivered with disarming cheerfulness, that the story had to be sold and that accuracy was one input among several.

Placing these four in conversation — rather than in confrontation — is the film's central method. The audience is invited to notice that Mignini and Pisa, who would never describe themselves as allies, produce compatible narratives. The prosecutor needs a monster; the tabloid needs a saleable monster. Knox needs to be a person. The film's argument emerges from that geometry rather than from any explicit thesis statement.

## 3. The tabloid gaze as the film's true antagonist

The strongest passages concern the mechanics of the coverage rather than the crime. The film shows how details were selected for their erotic or occult charge — the "Foxy Knoxy" nickname, the speculation about a sex game, the fixation on Knox's behaviour after the murder: kissing Sollecito outside the cottage, doing cartwheels at the police station.

The documentary's implicit claim is that these behaviours were read as *signs* because a story already existed that required them to be signs. A grieving person is expected to perform grief in a legible way; anything outside that script becomes suspicious. This is a genuinely useful observation, and the film makes it without over-explaining.

Where the film is less rigorous is in its own use of the same apparatus. It is beautifully cut, scored with persistent tension by Danny Bensi and Saunder Jurriaans, and paced like a thriller. It also withholds the ending from the audience for most of its runtime, so that the acquittal arrives as narrative release. These are the tools of the genre the film criticises. The film knows this and mostly gets away with it, but it is worth naming: a documentary about the danger of storytelling is still storytelling.

## 4. What the film leaves out

The most common criticism of *Amanda Knox* is that it is an advocacy film wearing the costume of neutrality. The criticism has force.

The forensic dimension is compressed almost to nothing. The disputed DNA evidence on the bra clasp and the knife, the independent review that undermined the prosecution's forensic case, the conviction of Rudy Guede in a separate fast-track trial — these are mentioned or implied rather than examined. A viewer who wants to understand *why* the case collapsed legally will not find the answer here; they will find the answer to a different question, namely *why* it became a spectacle.

The film also gives Knox the last word, and gives her a sympathetic, articulate one. She is a compelling interviewee, and the film is candid about the fact that it likes her. But liking her is not the same as establishing her innocence, and the film occasionally allows the two to blur. The result is a documentary that is honest about its sympathies in tone but not always in structure.

## 5. Reception and assessment

Critical reception was favourable but not ecstatic. *Amanda Knox* holds an 82% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes based on 50 reviews with an average score of 7.0/10, and a Metacritic weighted average of 78/100 from 17 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. The Rotten Tomatoes critics' consensus describes it as "an absorbing — if slightly incomplete — look at the details and troubling implications of her arrest and subsequent fight for freedom." That word, *incomplete*, is the right one.

As a piece of media criticism, the film is close to excellent: controlled, formally intelligent, and unusually clear-eyed about how a person becomes a story. As a piece of criminal-case journalism it is thin, and it is aware of being thin without doing much about it.

## 6. Conclusion

*Amanda Knox* is best understood not as an investigation but as a correction of the record at the level of tone. It cannot give the audience the evidence; it can give them the experience of being misread at scale, which is what the tabloid coverage did to its subject. Judged on that narrower ambition it succeeds. Judged as the definitive account of the Kercher case, it does not attempt the job — and the film's own reception, warm but qualified, registers exactly that gap.
